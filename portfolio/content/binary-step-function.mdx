The binary step function is one of the simplest activation functions in machine learning. In this article, I explain how it maps values to either `0` or `1`, why that makes it easy to reason about, and where it fits in the history of neural networks.

The piece also highlights its biggest limitation: the derivative is effectively zero, which makes gradient-based learning difficult. That tradeoff is exactly why more expressive activation functions became standard in deeper networks.

If you're starting with activation functions and want the intuition before the math gets heavier, this is a good place to begin.
