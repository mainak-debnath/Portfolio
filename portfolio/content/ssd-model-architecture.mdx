In this article, I explore the **Single Shot Multibox Detector (SSD)** architecture for object detection. The focus is on why SSD is considered a single-shot detector, how default bounding boxes work, and how multi-scale feature maps help it detect objects at different sizes.

I also compare SSD with YOLO to show the practical tradeoffs between speed, detection quality, and deployment choices. The goal was to make the architecture approachable without losing the system-level intuition behind it.
